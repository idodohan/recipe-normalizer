City Council Approves New Riverside Park Plan

By Dana Okafor · May 2, 2026

MILLTOWN — After nearly two years of contentious public hearings, the city council voted 6–1 on Thursday night to approve the $14.2 million redevelopment of Riverside Park, clearing the way for construction to begin this autumn.

The plan replaces the flood-damaged boathouse with an elevated pavilion, adds a half-mile of accessible riverfront boardwalk, and restores three acres of native wetland along the eastern bank. A controversial proposal to add paid parking was dropped from the final version after sustained opposition from neighborhood groups.

"This is the largest investment in our parks system in a generation," Mayor Elena Ruiz said after the vote. "It would not have happened without residents showing up, meeting after meeting, for two years."

Councilmember Gerald Hobbes, who cast the lone dissenting vote, said he supported the park improvements but objected to the financing structure, which relies on a 20-year bond. "We are mortgaging tomorrow's maintenance budget for today's ribbon cutting," he said.

The parks department estimates the first phase — demolition of the boathouse and stabilization of the riverbank — will take eight months. The full project is scheduled for completion in spring 2029.

Public art for the new pavilion will be selected through an open call this summer, with a budget of $180,000 set aside from the city's percent-for-art ordinance.

Residents can review the final site plan at the public library or on the city website. A groundbreaking ceremony is expected in late September.
